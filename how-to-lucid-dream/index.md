  <p class="guide-lead">If you want to learn how to lucid dream, the short version is this: improve dream recall, question your state during the day, and use methods like MILD and Wake Back to Bed consistently enough for your sleeping mind to notice.</p>

<section class="guide-section">
  <h2>Quick Answer</h2>
  <p>Lucid dreaming usually becomes more likely when you train three things together: memory for dreams, awareness during the day, and a reliable induction practice at night. Most beginners do best by starting with a dream journal, reality checks, MILD, and Wake Back to Bed.</p>
</section>

<section class="guide-section">
  <h2>Five Practical Steps</h2>
  <ol class="guide-steps">
    <li><strong>Record your dreams every morning.</strong> Better dream recall gives you material to work with and helps your brain treat dreams as important.</li>
    <li><strong>Notice recurring dream signs.</strong> Look for patterns like strange locations, impossible events, or repeated people.</li>
    <li><strong>Practice reality checks with attention.</strong> Ask whether you are dreaming and actually examine your surroundings instead of repeating the habit mechanically.</li>
    <li><strong>Use MILD before sleep or after waking in the night.</strong> Set the intention to recognize the dream the next time it begins.</li>
    <li><strong>Experiment with Wake Back to Bed.</strong> Briefly waking up and returning to sleep often makes lucid dreams more accessible.</li>
  </ol>
</section>
